Leap to Evidence

From assumptions to interviews

Every new business depends on beliefs nobody has checked yet, called assumptions. This tool helps you list your assumptions, decide which to test first, and plan how to test each one, for example with customer discovery interviews.

01Where your idea started

Where your idea started affects which of its assumptions are riskiest.

It started with...
Watch for the Innovator's Bias. When you start from a technology or product, it's easy to describe problems that justify building it. "When you've already decided to build a hammer, everything looks like a nail" (Ash Maurya). Listing several market opportunities below is one check on this bias.

Your market opportunities

A market opportunity is one application for one group of customers. The same product sold to different customers counts as different opportunities: a dog back brace for owners of breeds prone to back problems is one; the same brace for veterinary rehab clinics is another.

With a platform technology, finding opportunities is its own step. Use Where to Play (Marc Gruber and Sharon Tal; free online tool and worksheets) to find and compare them.

Enter your main opportunity first, then any others you're keeping open.

The tool uses this list to judge what happens when an assumption turns out false. A backup opportunity helps only if it doesn't depend on the failed assumption; Where to Play defines backups the same way.

02List your assumptions

An assumption is anything your business needs to be true that you haven't checked yet. Go box by box. Write fast; you can fix wording later.

Rate each assumption honestly. Leap of faith: gut instinct. Anecdote: you heard it from a few people or saw it once. Fact: you have data from real behavior. Gut instinct is fine; just label it.

03Your testing order

Calculated from your answers, not from how important things feel.

How the order is calculated

Consequence. If a technology assumption fails, every opportunity fails: Stop. If a customer or problem assumption fails, the tool checks your other opportunities. If at least one doesn't depend on it: Pivot. If all of them do: Stop. Money assumptions (price, channels, costs) are judged with a money model in stage 2.

Reach. How many of your other assumptions depend on this one. On the canvas, later boxes depend on earlier ones: problems belong to a customer, prices depend on the problem, channels depend on who the customer is.

Evidence. Leap-of-faith assumptions go before anecdotes; facts go last.

Earlier links first. If an earlier assumption is still a leap of faith, a "no" on a later assumption could mean only that the earlier assumption was wrong, so the later assumption waits. Customer and problem assumptions are tested together, and technology tests run alongside interviews.

    Check this order with an advisor or coach. Before you interview customers, show your canvas and this list to someone with experience in the industry or in starting companies. In step 05, choose "Advisor or coach" for a conversation plan.

    When to stop

    In qualitative research, interviews with similar people stop producing new issues after 9 to 17 interviews, and understanding those issues in depth takes more interviews (Hennink, Kaiser and Marconi, 2017; Hennink and Kaiser, 2022). Count only interviews with the same kind of person, recruited from different places.
